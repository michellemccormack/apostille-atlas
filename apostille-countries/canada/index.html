Apostille for Canada

Canada is a Contracting Party to the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention, in force there since January 11, 2024.[source] A U.S. public document headed to Canada generally needs an apostille from the U.S. office that covers the document, not embassy legalization.

Sending a U.S. document to Canada

The apostille comes from the U.S. jurisdiction that issued or notarized the document, not from Canada. Under the U.S. designation filed with the Hague Conference:[source]

  • State documents (birth, marriage, and death certificates, state court records, documents signed by a notary in that state): the competent authority of that state, generally the Secretary of State. See who issues apostilles in each state.
  • Federal documents (documents issued by U.S. federal agencies): the U.S. Department of State Office of Authentications.
  • U.S. federal court documents: the clerk or a deputy clerk of the federal court that issued them.
  • Consular Reports of Birth, Death, and Marriage Abroad: the State Department’s Passport Services, Vital Records Section.

The office in Canada that receives your document can still set its own rules about translations, how recent the document must be, and whether it accepts a notarized copy. Ask before you order the apostille.
