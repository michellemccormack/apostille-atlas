Apostille for Hungary

Hungary is a Contracting Party to the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention, in force there since January 18, 1973.[source] A U.S. public document headed to Hungary generally needs an apostille from the U.S. office that covers the document, not embassy legalization.

Sending a U.S. document to Hungary

The apostille comes from the U.S. jurisdiction that issued or notarized the document, not from Hungary. Under the U.S. designation filed with the Hague Conference:[source]

  • State documents (birth, marriage, and death certificates, state court records, documents signed by a notary in that state): the competent authority of that state, generally the Secretary of State. See who issues apostilles in each state.
  • Federal documents (documents issued by U.S. federal agencies): the U.S. Department of State Office of Authentications.
  • U.S. federal court documents: the clerk or a deputy clerk of the federal court that issued them.
  • Consular Reports of Birth, Death, and Marriage Abroad: the State Department’s Passport Services, Vital Records Section.

The office in Hungary that receives your document can still set its own rules about translations, how recent the document must be, and whether it accepts a notarized copy. Ask before you order the apostille.

Using a document from Hungary in the U.S.

A public document issued in Hungary gets its apostille from Hungary’s own competent authority. This is what Hungary has registered with the HCCH:
