Apostille Atlas

A state-by-state guide to U.S. apostilles and authentication certificates: the official fee, the issuing office, mail versus in-person filing, and whatever processing time that office actually publishes.

An apostille is a certificate that authenticates the signature on a public document so the document can be used in another country that belongs to the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention. The Hague Conference describes the Convention as replacing the longer legalization chain with a single apostille issued by a competent authority where the document originates.[source]

New York

$10 per document at the Department of State. Same-day walk-in service, limited to 10 documents, at five customer-service offices. No published mail turnaround.

California

$20 per apostille by mail; in person add $6 special handling per public official’s signature. Sacramento was processing mail received November 12, 2026, as of November 20, 2026.

Texas

$15 per universal apostille ($10, capped at $100, for an adoption). Same-day in-person service for up to 10 documents; mailed requests can take up to two weeks from receipt.

Florida

$10 per document, or $20 when a clerk-of-court certification needs a certificate of incumbency. Walk-in service in Tallahassee; as of November 20, 2026, apostille requests received November 16, 2026 were being processed.

Massachusetts

$6 per document. Up to three documents at the Boston counter; mailed requests are processed within two to three weeks. Regional offices in Springfield and Fall River.

New Jersey

$25 standard or $40 expedited per document, ordered and prepaid online first. Standard mail service takes 12 to 20 business days; walk-in expedited service in Trenton.

Virginia

$10 per document, $5 for extra same-signer documents. Documents must be dated within 12 months. Richmond is appointment-only, and eNotary documents can’t be authenticated.

North Carolina

$10 per document. Walk-in service in Raleigh with no appointment; mailed requests generally take five business days longer. Foreign-language documents need a wet-signed English translation.

Pennsylvania

$15 per document. Walk-in requests in Harrisburg are processed while you wait; mail takes 5 to 7 business days after receipt. Diplomas, transcripts, and criminal record checks must be notarized first.

Missouri

$10 per notary or official on the document, capped at $100 per child for adoptions. Same-day walk-in service in Jefferson City and branch offices in St. Louis, Kansas City, and Springfield.

Washington

$15 per document. Standard 7 to 10 business days, expedited 2 to 3 for $100 per 10 documents, or same day in Tumwater or Cheney for $150 per first 10.

Michigan

$1 per document from the Office of the Great Seal. Mail to Lansing or book a visit at one of nine offices; no expedited service. Diplomas and copies need a signed “true copy” statement before a Michigan notary.

Connecticut

Every order starts online. Paper apostilles take 5 to 7 business days after arrival in Hartford; e-Apostilles, about 24 hours. Documents must be signed or notarized in Connecticut within the last 10 years.

Maryland

$5 per document. Notarized papers go to the Clerk of the Circuit Court in the notary’s county first. Mail takes 7 to 14 business days by regular mail, about 3 to 4 by courier or Priority Mail.

Georgia

$3 per document from the GSCCCA in Atlanta, not the Secretary of State. Walk in with no appointment or mail it; mailed requests normally take one to two business days. Non-Hague countries need the Secretary of State’s Great Seal Certification.

Colorado

$5 per apostille by mail or drop-off, $15 for expedited walk-in service in Denver, generally under an hour. Check or money order only; free document review by email before you submit.

Minnesota

$5 per document, by mail or in-person appointment in Saint Paul. Minnesota issues only apostilles, for every country, and notarizations must show all six elements of Minnesota law.

Arizona

$3 per document from the Secretary of State. Mail takes 10 to 20 business days; walk-in service in Phoenix or Tucson adds a $25 expedite fee and is limited to 6 documents. No electronic apostilles.

How to verify an apostille

Only the issuing office can confirm an apostille. The 17 U.S. authorities with an online register listed by the Hague Conference, and what to do when there is no online search.

Does an apostille expire?

No: under the Convention an apostille can’t be refused for its age. What can run out is the receiving country’s time limit on the document underneath.

Apostille versus authentication

Hague Convention countries generally use an apostille. Non-Hague countries often need a different certificate, and sometimes a further step at the U.S. Department of State. States do not all issue the same kind of paper.

Online notarization (RON)

A remote online notary can notarize a document, but the apostille still comes from the state competent authority. Texas publishes a specific RON checklist; some countries still refuse online notarizations.
