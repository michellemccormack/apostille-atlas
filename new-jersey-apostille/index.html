How to get an apostille in New Jersey

Short answer: In New Jersey, apostilles come from the Treasury's Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services, not a Secretary of State. Every request starts with a prepaid online order: $25 per document for standard service (12 to 20 business days) or $40 expedited (about 8.5 business hours), plus a card or e-check fee. Then send the original. [source][source]

In New Jersey, apostilles and certifications come from the Department of the Treasury, Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services, not from a Secretary of State. Every request starts with an online order that you prepay, and you then mail or hand-deliver the original document with the printed order confirmation.[source][source]

Step by step

  1. Get the right original. You need the original or a certified copy of the document that carries the original notarization or the original certification of the public official.[source]
  2. Check the notarization. A notarized document must carry the original (inked) signature of the New Jersey notary public and the original (inked) signature of the person who presented the document for notarization.[source]
  3. Place the online order. The order asks for your contact details, a description of each document (for example the state registrar, the notary’s commission ID, and the date of the event), the processing level, how you want the documents returned, and payment.[source] You must place a separate online order for each country where you will present the documents.[source]
  4. Send the original with the confirmation. Print the order confirmation that is emailed to you and send it with the original documents, as described below.[source]

Where do you send the document?

ServiceHow to deliver
Standard (by mail)Mail the originals and the printed confirmation by USPS first class, Priority, or Certified Mail to Division of Revenue and Enterprise Services, PO Box 452, Trenton, NJ 08646.[source]
Expedited (in person)Customer Service Counter, 33 West State Street, 5th floor, Trenton, NJ 08608, between 8:30 a.m. and 2:00 p.m. If you deliver in person, you can wait for the apostille; the wait can be up to 2.5 hours, and walk-in service depends on workload, so the office suggests calling 609-292-9292 before you go.[source]
Expedited (courier)Send it by an express delivery service such as UPS or FedEx to the same 33 West State Street address. The US Postal Service is not available for expedited requests.[source]

Which documents will New Jersey certify?

The Division certifies the signatures of New Jersey notaries public and selected public officials, such as Superior Court judges, county clerks, and the State Registrar of Vital Statistics.[source]

It issues an apostille when the document is going to a country that belongs to the Hague Convention. For a country that is not on the Hague list, it issues a certification of the public official instead. See apostille versus authentication.[source]

Birth, marriage, and other vital records

Vital records (birth, marriage, divorce, death, and so on) from January 1, 1923 forward come from the New Jersey Department of Health, Office of Vital Statistics and Registry (609-292-4087). For records dated before January 1, 1923, contact the New Jersey State Archives.[source]

New Jersey cannot issue apostilles or certifications for vital records from other states. Those must be authenticated in the state where they were issued.[source]

Documents in another language

For documents that are not in English, the Division recommends attaching a notarized English translation. If you attach a notarized translation to a vital record, it issues two apostilles or certifications and charges two fees.[source]
