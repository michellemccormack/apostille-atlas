How to get an apostille in Georgia

Short answer: In Georgia, apostilles are issued by the Georgia Superior Court Clerks’ Cooperative Authority (GSCCCA), not the Secretary of State. It charges $3 per document. You can walk in at 1875 Century Blvd. in Atlanta, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays with no appointment, or mail your originals; mailed requests normally take one to two business days.[source]

Georgia splits the work between two agencies. The GSCCCA issues apostilles for countries in the Hague Apostille Convention. For a country that isn’t in the Convention, the Georgia Secretary of State issues a Great Seal Certification instead; the GSCCCA can’t.[source] The GSCCCA is the designated apostille authority for Georgia on the Hague Conference’s list for the United States.[source]

Atlanta is the only place in Georgia that issues apostilles. The GSCCCA doesn’t notarize documents, so arrive with the notarization already done.[source]

Apostille or Great Seal Certification?

Start with the destination country. If it is on the GSCCCA’s list of participating countries, the document goes to the GSCCCA for an apostille. If it isn’t listed, the country isn’t part of the Apostille Convention and you need the Secretary of State’s Great Seal Certification.[source] A document going to a foreign embassy or consulate inside the United States counts as going to that country.[source]

The GSCCCA also issues a certification of notary public ($2), but that one is only for documents used inside the U.S., a U.S. territory, or a U.S. embassy, typically for visa requests. It is never used together with an apostille.[source] For how the two foreign certificates differ, see apostille versus authentication.

The GSCCCA checks the official’s signature against the ones it has on file. If your vital record was issued before 2000, it recommends getting a new certified copy, and it prefers copies issued since then.[source] New certified birth and death certificates come from the Georgia Department of Community Health (Vital Records) or the county Probate Court or Health Department.[source]

What it can’t apostille

  • Documents from another state; send them to that state.[source]
  • Federal documents signed and sealed by a federal agency or court, including a consular report of birth abroad. These go to the U.S. Department of State.[source]
  • Photocopies, unless a Georgia notary has properly certified the copy.[source]
  • Foreign documents, documents for non-Hague countries, and documents for use inside the United States.[source]

What the notarization must show

The notary completes a notarial certificate and signs by hand in ink, using exactly the name on the commission, with the exact date of notarization. The seal must show the notary’s name, the county of commission, the words “Notary Public,” and the state, and the impression has to be clear.[source] Sample certificates are in the GSCCCA’s notary files and forms. If you plan to notarize online, the document still has to be a Georgia notarization for the GSCCCA to apostille it.

Specific documents

  • Transcripts and grades: a school official signs in front of a notary. The simplest route is a short letter from the official saying the attached are the student’s official records. Only Georgia school records can be apostilled in Georgia.[source]
  • Diplomas: a Georgia notary can certify a copy of the original as a “true and exact copy of the original,” and the apostille goes on that copy.[source]
  • Background checks: an FBI check signed and sealed by an FBI official goes to the U.S. Department of State. A GBI check signed by a GBI official before a notary is ready for the apostille. For an unsigned FBI check or a local police check that wasn’t notarized, you sign the GSCCCA’s Criminal Records Check affidavit before a Georgia notary.[source]
  • Translations: the translator signs an affidavit before a Georgia notary saying they know both languages and the translation is true and complete. The notary can’t also be the translator. The original document doesn’t need to be in English.[source]
  • Passport copies: a Georgia notary can certify a copy of a U.S. passport using the GSCCCA’s passport affidavit; a foreign passport copy uses its Foreign Passport Affidavit.[source]
  • Getting married abroad: there’s no standard U.S. certificate of freedom to marry, and a notarized single status affidavit often works; the GSCCCA has sample language.[source]
  • Adoptions: the GSCCCA encourages adoptive parents to contact it before submitting.[source]

What to put in a mailed request

  1. The original documents.[source]
  2. A cover letter or note naming the destination country, with your contact information. The GSCCCA has a sample Apostille Request Letter in its forms.[source]
  3. $3 per document by check or money order payable to GSCCCA, or a credit card pre-payment voucher.[source]
  4. A prepaid, self-addressed return envelope or airbill. FedEx and UPS airbills must be computer generated; handwritten ones are not accepted.[source]
