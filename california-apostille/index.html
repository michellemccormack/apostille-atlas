How to get an apostille in California

Short answer: The California Secretary of State charges $20 per apostille. In person in Sacramento or Los Angeles, add a $6 special handling fee per official's signature; most requests are done within about 30 minutes. Mailed requests go only to Sacramento, are handled in the order received and can't be expedited, so check the posted processing date first. [source][source]

The California Secretary of State issues an apostille that authenticates a California public official’s signature on a document to be used outside the United States. The office issues a single apostille regardless of the destination country; it does not issue a separate authentication certificate.

What do you include in a mailed request?

Four items are required for a mail request:[source]

  1. The document that needs the apostille. It must be signed by a California public official, or be an original notarized and/or certified document. A photocopy is not acceptable.
  2. A cover sheet stating the country in which the document will be used (the office’s mail cover sheet, or your own).
  3. A check or money order payable to Secretary of State for $20.00 per apostille.
  4. A self-addressed envelope for return. Provide prepaid postage if you want a tracked service; otherwise the office returns the document by regular U.S. mail.

Anyone may request an apostille in person, including on behalf of someone else, and the requester does not need to be related to a person named in the document.[source]

Payment in Sacramento may be by credit card (Visa or Mastercard), check, money order, or cash. The Los Angeles office accepts credit card (Visa or Mastercard), check, or money order; cash is not accepted there.[source]

Vital records

For California birth and death certificates, the Secretary of State can issue an apostille for the signatures of county clerks and their deputies, county recorders and their deputies, and the State Registrar at the California Department of Public Health. If a city- or county-level certificate is signed by a health officer or county registrar, the official FAQ says you may first need a county-clerk certification or a certified copy from the county recorder or the state Department of Public Health.[source]
