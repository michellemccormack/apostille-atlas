How to get an apostille in Minnesota

Short answer: The Minnesota Secretary of State charges $5 per document for an apostille. Mail the documents with the request form and payment to the First National Bank Building, 332 Minnesota Street, Suite N201, Saint Paul, MN 55101, or book an in-person appointment. Birth, marriage, and other certified records go as they are; everything else must be notarized by a Minnesota notary first.[source]

Minnesota issues only one kind of certificate: the apostille, whatever the destination country. If the receiving country doesn’t accept an apostille, contact that country’s embassy or consulate about your options.[source] An apostille is for international use only and can’t be used in the United States or its territories.[source]

Two kinds of documents

Your documentWhat it needs
State or county certified records: birth and death certificates, marriage certificates, divorce decrees, judgmentsNo notarization. It must be issued in Minnesota, be the original certified document (most county-issued documents have an embossed seal), and not be a photocopy.[source]
Everything else: adoption papers, affidavits, diplomas, transcripts, powers of attorneyProperly notarized by a Minnesota notary public[source]

Documents that originated outside Minnesota are a common reason for rejection, as are private documents that weren’t notarized and documents without an original signature.[source]

The six things a Minnesota notarization must show

Minnesota checks notarized documents against Minnesota Statutes 358.65. All six elements must be there:[source]

  1. Jurisdiction: where the notarization took place, for example County of Ramsey, State of Minnesota.
  2. Notarial statement: what the notary witnessed, such as a signature, an oath, or a true copy.
  3. Title: stated as Notary Public.
  4. Date of the notarization.
  5. Notary signature in wet ink, not a photocopy.
  6. Notary stamp.

A missing certificate and an invalid notary commission, including a stamp that doesn’t match, are the notarization problems the office lists most often.[source] If you plan to notarize online, choose a notary commissioned in Minnesota, because documents must be notarized by a Minnesota notary public.[source]

What to put in a mailed request

  1. The Apostille/Authentication Request Form, with the number of documents (you need one document for each apostille).[source]
  2. The original certified or notarized documents.[source]
  3. $5 per document, payable to MN Secretary of State.[source]
  4. Your return choice. You can pick up from the office, but payment must be in the envelope (you can’t pay at pickup), and if you don’t confirm within three business days the order is mailed by regular U.S. mail. Otherwise enclose a prepaid return envelope, or the documents come back by regular U.S. mail, which has no tracking.[source]

The Secretary of State is the designated apostille authority for Minnesota on the Hague Conference’s list for the United States.[source] For the destination country, see apostille by country, and for countries outside the Convention, see apostille versus authentication.
