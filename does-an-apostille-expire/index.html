Does an apostille expire?

Short answer: No. Under the Hague Apostille Convention, a validly issued apostille stays in effect for as long as it can be identified and remains attached to its document, and it can’t be rejected just because of its age. But the country receiving the document can set its own time limit on the document underneath, for example requiring a record issued within a certain period. When people say an apostille “expired,” that is usually what happened.[source]

The Hague Conference’s Apostille Handbook (second edition, 2023) is the official practical guide to how the Convention works. It addresses the age question in three places: the general rule, the list of grounds on which an apostille may not be rejected, and the section on e-Apostilles.[source][source]

What the Convention rule actually says

QuestionAnswer from the Apostille Handbook
Does the apostille itself have an end date?No. It has effect for as long as it is identifiable and stays attached to the underlying public document.[source]
Can the receiving country reject an apostille as too old?No. An apostille may not be rejected on the basis of its age.[source]
Can the receiving country require a recent document?Yes. Contracting Parties may set time limits on accepting foreign public documents, for example that the document be produced within a certain period after it was executed. Those limits can’t be applied to the apostille itself.[source]
Can an expired document be apostilled?Usually. Some documents carry their own validity period (criminal records, identity and travel documents, provisional court orders). Expiry doesn’t ordinarily stop them being public documents unless the law of the issuing country says so, and as long as it is still a public document it can be apostilled.[source]
Does an e-Apostille expire when the signer’s digital certificate does?No. An e-Apostille stays valid after the signing official’s digital certificate expires, as long as that certificate was valid when the e-Apostille was issued.[source]

The reason the rule works this way: an apostille only certifies where a public document came from, meaning the signature, the capacity of the person who signed it, and any seal or stamp. It doesn’t certify the content.[source] Whether the content is current enough is a separate question for the receiving authority, which keeps the right to decide whether a foreign document is admissible and what weight it carries.[source]

Where age limits usually bite

The time limit almost always sits on the document, not the certificate. The Handbook itself names criminal records among the documents that carry their own validity period.[source] In practice that means:

  • Criminal record checks carry their own validity period.[source] If the receiving office sets a limit on how recent the record must be, ask what it is before you order the check, so the time spent getting the apostille doesn’t use up that window.
  • Vital records (birth, marriage, death) are a matter for the receiving office’s rules too, and occasionally for the issuing state’s. Georgia’s apostille office, which checks the official’s signature against those on file, recommends a new certified copy if a vital record was issued before 2000.[source]
  • An old apostille on a still-acceptable document is fine. If an official refuses it only because the apostille is old, that refusal isn’t consistent with the Convention.[source]

If the receiving office does want a newer document, you need a fresh certified copy (or a new notarization) and a new apostille from the state that issued it; the old apostille can’t be moved onto the new paper, since it only has effect while attached to its own document.[source] Our apostille finder gives the office and fee for each covered state.

Before you assume it has expired

  1. Ask the receiving authority what it actually requires. If it wants a recently issued document, that is a rule about the document, not the apostille.[source]
  2. Check the apostille is still attached and legible, since its effect depends on staying identifiable and attached.[source]
  3. If the receiving office doubts the apostille is genuine, point it to the issuing office’s register; see how to verify an apostille.
  4. If the destination isn’t a Hague country at all, the apostille rules don’t apply; see apostille versus authentication and apostille by country.
