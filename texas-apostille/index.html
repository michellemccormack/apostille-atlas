How to get an apostille in Texas

Short answer: The Texas Secretary of State's Authentications Unit in Austin charges $15 per universal apostille ($10 for adoption documents, capped at $100 per child). It works as an apostille for Hague countries and as an authentication for others. In-person service is same-day for up to 10 documents; mailed requests can take up to two weeks. [source][source]

The Texas Secretary of State, Authentications Unit, issues a “universal Apostille.” Officially, that certificate operates as an apostille for countries that are party to the Apostille Convention and as an authentication for countries that are not.

Hours and payment

The walk-in office is listed at 400 West 15th Street, Austin, TX 78701, 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., Monday through Friday. The request page warns that in-person wait times may exceed an hour, and that the office was closed Wednesday, November 25, through Friday, November 27, 2026, for Thanksgiving — confirm current closures before you go.[source]

Walk-in payment may be by check, money order, credit or debit card, or cash with exact change. Mailed requests may be paid by check or money order drawn on a U.S. bank and payable to the Office of the Texas Secretary of State, or by funding an SOS client account. The FAQ says checks and money orders should be in favor of the “Secretary of State of Texas.” Personal checks or money orders missing the amount, the requestor’s complete address, or the payee’s signature will not be accepted.[source][source]

Which documents qualify for a Texas apostille?

All documents must be issued from the state of Texas or properly notarized by a Texas notary public. You must submit the original Texas-issued document, a Texas-issued certified copy, or an original Texas-notarized document. The office does not apostille copies of certified or notarized records, and it does not apostille documents for use within the United States or its territories. Certified records must have been issued within the past five years; the official page says documents older than five years may not be verifiable and cannot be apostilled.[source]

You must indicate the destination country on Form 2102.[source]

If the destination country is not a party to the Apostille Convention, the official FAQ says you “likely need to get your universal Apostille validated by the Office of Authentications with the U.S. State Department.”[source]

Can Texas apostille a remote online (RON) notarization?

Texas will apostille an online (RON) notarization if you also present a notarization ledger (date and time signatures were applied, document description, and signer information) and a notarial certificate stating the document was notarized via “electronic or online notarization by two-way audio and visual communication.” The official page warns that some countries do not accept online notarized documents even after this office authenticates them.[source] See online notarization for apostille documents.
