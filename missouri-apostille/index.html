How to get an apostille in Missouri

Short answer: The Missouri Secretary of State charges $10 per apostille, authentication, or certification, counted per notary or official on the document. Walk-in customers in Jefferson City can get same-day processing, and the St. Louis, Kansas City, and Springfield branch offices provide the service too. Mailed documents are given same-day or next-day service, according to the state’s Notary Handbook. [source][handbook]

The Secretary of State’s Notaries & Commissions Unit confirms that a Missouri official, such as a notary public, recorder of deeds, circuit clerk, judge, county clerk, or the State Registrar, had the authority to sign your document. The country where the document will be used decides which certificate is attached.[source]

How the $10 fee is counted

Missouri charges by the official being verified, document by document. The Secretary of State gives these examples:[source]

  • One document with several notarizations, all by the same notary: $10.
  • Several documents, all notarized by the same notary: $10 per document.
  • One document notarized by several different notaries: $10 per notary.

Apostille, authentication, or certification?

CertificateWhat it verifies
CertificationA Missouri notary public.
AuthenticationA recorder of deeds, circuit clerk, judge, county clerk, or the State Registrar (for example on a birth certificate, marriage statement, or divorce statement).
ApostilleThe same officials, for a country that has joined the Hague Apostille Convention. No authentication above the Secretary of State is needed.

The country of use determines which of these the office attaches.[source] The handbook adds that the apostille must be requested for documents going to member countries.[handbook] Check your destination in apostille by country, and see apostille versus authentication for what happens after a non-Hague authentication.

Preparing each document

DocumentWhat Missouri needs
Birth certificateA certified copy from the Bureau of Vital Records, (573) 751-6387.[source][Vital Records]
Marriage licenseA certified copy from the Recorder of Deeds in the county of the marriage, or a statement related to marriage from the Bureau of Vital Records.[source]
Divorce decreeA certified copy from the Circuit Clerk of the county where the divorce is recorded.[source]
Diplomas and transcriptsA certified copy from the school. The registrar or another school authority signs a statement typed on the back of the diploma or transcript saying it is the original record (or a copy of it) and the date issued, and a notary witnesses that signature. Don’t alter the face of the diploma.[source][handbook]
Notarized documentsA complete notarization: the notarial paragraph, the notary’s signature, and the seal.[source]

Missouri notaries can’t certify copies of birth certificates, death certificates, divorce decrees, or marriage licenses; those must come certified from the issuing agency. Missouri notaries also can’t certify documents from other states or countries.[handbook]

Step by step

  1. Get the document certified or notarized as shown above.[source]
  2. Fill out the cover letter with your contact details, the number of documents, the country of use, and return instructions.[source]
  3. Add payment at $10 per apostille or authentication, or the credit card payment sheet.[source]
  4. Plan the return. Documents come back by regular mail unless you include a prepaid envelope with an addressed air bill. If you pay by card and send documents by special delivery, include a completed air bill.[source]

The office will also forward documents straight to a third party, such as a consulate, if you enclose a stamped envelope addressed to them.[handbook]

Electronic notarizations

Missouri law provides for an electronic certificate of authority for an electronic notarial act, including an electronic form of the apostille, with a fee of up to $10 (section 486.975, reprinted in the Notary Handbook).[handbook] If you are thinking about notarizing online first, read online notarization for apostille documents, and confirm with the receiving country that it accepts an electronically notarized document.
