How to get an apostille in Pennsylvania

Short answer: The Pennsylvania Department of State’s Office of Notaries, Commissions and Legislation in Harrisburg charges $15 per document (not per page). Walk-in requests at Room 201 of the North Office Building are processed while you wait; by mail, the services page says 5 to 7 business days after receipt, not counting mailing time. [source]

The Secretary of the Commonwealth authenticates the signatures of Pennsylvania public officials on documents that will be used outside the United States. The destination country decides whether you get an apostille or a certification, and neither is issued for documents used only inside the U.S.[source]

Apostille or certification?

For a country that belongs to the 1961 Hague Apostille Convention, the department issues an apostille, which needs no further diplomatic or consular legalization. For other countries it attaches a certification, which may still need authentication by the U.S. Department of State and the destination country’s embassy.[source] Tell the office exactly where the document is going: substituting one for the other delays your documents.[source]

Pennsylvania now prints both on a single universal certificate, with Spanish and French translations of each section and an electronic seal in black ink instead of a raised gold seal. The department says any certificate can be verified by its certification number in its online document search.[source] For the general difference, see apostille versus authentication, and check your destination in apostille by country.

Step by step

  1. Get an original with the right signature. The document must be an original (no photocopies), signed by and carrying the seal of a Pennsylvania public official: a state elected or appointed official, a county elected official, a judge or magisterial district judge, or a Pennsylvania notary public.[source][source]
  2. Fill out the request. Use a cover letter or the department’s Request for Legalization of Documents form (also in Chinese and Spanish). Give the destination country and the requester’s name, phone, email, and full mailing address.[source]
  3. Pay $15 per document. One check or money order to “Commonwealth of Pennsylvania.” Checks from a foreign bank must be in U.S. dollars and carry a routing number.[source]
  4. Add a return envelope. Enclose a self-addressed, stamped envelope, or a prepaid air bill for courier return. For UPS, list yourself as both sender and recipient. FedEx and DHL are not accepted for return shipments.[source]
  5. Walk in or mail it. Walk-in customers are served first come, first served; tell the counter how many documents (not pages) and the destination country.[source]

Which signature does each document need?

DocumentPennsylvania rule
Birth certificateDoes not need to be, and should not be, notarized.[source] Birth and death records come from the Pennsylvania Department of Health, Vital Records.[source]
Death certificateMust be signed by the state registrar. Certificates signed by a local registrar are not accepted.[source]
Marriage certificateMust be signed by the register of wills or the clerk of the orphans’ court, not only the officiant or clergy. Marriage and divorce records come from the county office where they were filed.[source]
County documentsMust be signed by the clerk in charge of the county office. Documents signed by an assistant or deputy clerk are not accepted.[source]
Diplomas, transcripts, criminal record checksMust be notarized by a Pennsylvania notary public before you request the apostille or certification.[source]
Notarized documentsThe notarial certificate must be worded and completed in English. A version in another language the notary understands may appear immediately next to the English one.[source]
Business documentsArticles of incorporation, certificates of good standing, and bylaws are among the common examples the department authenticates.[source]
FBI checks and other federal documentsNot handled here; the department refers you to the U.S. Department of State.[source][U.S. State Dept.]

What gets a request held up?

  • Photocopies. Photocopies of notarized or certified documents can’t be certified.[source]
  • The wrong signer. A deputy county clerk, a local registrar on a death certificate, or only the officiant on a marriage certificate.[source]
  • No destination country, or the wrong fee. Both are on the department’s acceptance checklist.[source]
  • Removing the staple. Pennsylvania attaches its certificate only by staple. Don’t remove it after the document is processed.[source]

Certificate of Good Standing for a notary

If a Pennsylvania-notarized document is being filed in another state or country that wants proof of the notary’s appointment, the department issues a Certificate of Good Standing for Notary Public. Write a letter asking for one with the notary’s full name and commission ID if known, your name and mailing address, and $15 per certificate.[source]
