How to get an apostille in Maryland

Short answer: The Maryland Secretary of State’s Certification Desk in Annapolis charges $5 per document for an apostille or a standard certification. Mail is the dependable route right now: walk-in service is not available, scheduled appointments are subject to change because of building construction, and the office says it will move to mail-in only once construction begins. If the document is notarized, take it to the Clerk of the Circuit Court in the notary’s county first.[source]

Maryland issues an apostille for countries in the Hague Convention and a standard certification for all others. A standard certification then goes to the U.S. Department of State, Office of Authentications, before the destination country’s embassy.[source]

The two routes

Your documentSteps
Notarized document (power of attorney, affidavit, consent, school record)1. Take it to the Clerk of the Circuit Court in the county where the notary was commissioned (the county is on the notary’s seal). 2. Send it to the Secretary of State in Annapolis.[source]
State or county document (birth or death certificate, marriage certificate, divorce decree, criminal record status certification, motor vehicle ownership certification)Send it straight to the Secretary of State, as long as it carries the seal and authorized signature of the issuing department. No circuit court step. If your copy isn’t current, call 410-974-5521 first to confirm it can be authenticated.[source]
Federal document (FBI background check, USCIS documents, birth abroad certificates)Not Maryland’s. Submit it directly to the U.S. Department of State. Naturalization and immigration documents can’t be notarized, originals or copies.[source]

For a Hague country, the Secretary of State’s apostille is the last step; the document doesn’t go to the U.S. Department of State. For a non-Hague country, it goes to the U.S. Department of State after the Maryland certificate.[source]

What does the circuit court step cost?

Each county’s Clerk of the Circuit Court handles its own certification. In Montgomery County, for example, the Circuit Court’s License Department (50 Maryland Avenue, North Tower, Room 1300, Rockville) charges $1, with no appointment needed.[source] Check with the clerk in your notary’s county for its fee and hours. If the clerk who certified your document isn’t the current clerk, call the Secretary of State to confirm it can still be authenticated.[source]

Diplomas and transcripts

School records follow the notarized-document route. Schools often send records in a sealed envelope marked not to open. Take the sealed envelope to a notary, who opens it and notarizes the school record. Then the Clerk of the Circuit Court in the notary’s county certifies that the notary is in good standing, and the record goes to the Secretary of State.[source] If you have the record notarized online, the circuit court step still has to happen in the county where that notary is commissioned, so a Maryland-commissioned notary keeps this simple.

What to put in a mailed request

  1. The documents, already certified by the circuit court where needed.[source]
  2. The mailing checklist: your name, address, daytime phone, email, and the country where the documents will be used.[source]
  3. A check or money order to the Secretary of State for $5 per document.[source]
  4. A postage-paid, self-addressed return envelope, or a prepaid FedEx or UPS envelope. Keep your tracking numbers.[source]

Things to know

  • The Secretary of State can’t guarantee that a foreign country will accept the document; confirm requirements with the receiving office first.[source]
  • The Secretary of State’s authority to certify comes from State Government Article, §7-106.[source]
