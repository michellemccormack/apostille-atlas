How to get an apostille in North Carolina

Short answer: The North Carolina Secretary of State's Authentication Office in Raleigh charges $10 per document and issues an Apostille, an Authentication or an Authority certificate, depending on the country and the document. There is no online filing. Walk in at 2 South Salisbury Street with no appointment, or mail it; mail generally takes five business days longer. [source][source][source]

The North Carolina Secretary of State’s Authentication Office in Raleigh issues apostilles, authentications, and certificates for North Carolina documents, including birth and marriage certificates, statements of marital status, corporate documents, powers of attorney, diplomas, school transcripts, and deeds.[source]

Which certificate do you need?

North Carolina issues three different certificates, and which one you get depends on the destination country and the kind of document:[source]

CertificateUsed for
ApostilleDocuments going to countries that have signed the Hague Convention.
AuthenticationNorth Carolina government-issued documents going to countries that have not signed the Hague Convention.
AuthorityDocuments notarized in North Carolina going to countries that have not signed the Hague Convention.

Once you have an apostille, the document can go directly to the destination country without further certification from the U.S. Department of State.[source] For how the two systems differ, see apostille versus authentication.

Step by step

  1. Make sure it is in English. If the document is in another language, get a certified or notarized English translation.[source] Since late 2024, the English translation must carry a wet (original) signature.[source]
  2. Get the right signature on it. Sign before a North Carolina notary, or get a certified copy notarized by the issuing entity. For a copy of a North Carolina document, you can use a notarized “true copy” affidavit; the office provides a sample on its cover letters and templates page.[source]
  3. Prepare the cover letter. It needs a contact name, a daytime phone number, the country where the document will be used, and the correct fee.[source] The cover letter is on the office’s forms page.[source]
  4. Send or deliver it. Include the original documents with all certifications attached, the payment, and a prepaid return envelope or carrier label.[source]

Which documents don’t need a notary?

Documents from these North Carolina offices go straight to the Authentication Office without a notarization:[source]

  • Register of Deeds
  • Clerk of Superior Court
  • North Carolina Department of Agriculture
  • North Carolina Department of Health and Human Services
  • North Carolina Vital Records (phone 919-733-3526)
  • North Carolina Department of Motor Vehicles (Raleigh office only)
  • North Carolina Attorney General’s Office
  • North Carolina Legislative Services

Diplomas and transcripts

Transcripts, report cards, diplomas, and similar documents from a North Carolina university, college, community college, or high school must be notarized by a North Carolina notary public. A school seal applied by the registrar is not acceptable.[source]

What gets a request rejected?

  • Old signatures. The certifying official’s signature must be from April 2000 or later, because the office does not have official signatures on record from before 2000.[source][source]
  • Out-of-state documents. North Carolina can’t authenticate a document from another state.[source]
  • Missing translation. A foreign-language document without a certified or notarized English translation is denied.[source]
  • U.S. use. Documents to be used in the United States can’t be authenticated.[source]

Return shipping

Without a prepaid label or self-addressed stamped envelope, documents come back by regular mail. A prepaid courier label must be computer-printed and paid for when you print it; a handwritten label with an account number doesn’t count. The office rejects FedEx labels that list the Secretary of State with “Bill Sender.” UPS and FedEx air labels are picked up automatically; for ground labels, the office calls you and you schedule the pickup.[source][source]

For documents going back to a foreign address, the office no longer uses UPS, FedEx, or DHL. It will mail a USPS prepaid envelope and label to a foreign address as long as the sender address isn’t the Secretary of State’s.[source]

Emergencies can be considered for expedited processing if you send documentation of the emergency.[source]
