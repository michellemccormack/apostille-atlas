How to get an apostille in New York

Short answer: New York apostilles come from the Department of State's Apostille and Authentication Unit and cost $10 per document. Notarized papers and most county-issued vital records need a county clerk certification first. Mail the request to Albany, or use same-day walk-in service in New York City, Albany, Binghamton, Buffalo or Utica (up to 10 documents). [source]

The New York Department of State authenticates public documents issued in New York State that are signed by a New York State official or county clerk. The same office issues either an apostille or a certificate of authentication, depending on the destination country.

What has to happen before you file?

The Department of State only authenticates public documents issued in New York State and signed by a New York State official or county clerk.[source]

  • Birth, death, and marriage certificates and certificates of divorce issued by a local or county official, or a county department of health, must first be certified by the county clerk of the county where the document was issued.
  • Vital records issued by the New York State Department of Health and bearing the signature of the Director of Vital Statistics or the NYS Registrar may be submitted without a county-clerk certification. The official page warns that the words “New York State,” “registrar,” or “vital records” on a local certificate do not, by themselves, mean it was issued by the State Department of Health.
  • Notarized documents must be certified by the county clerk of the county where the notary is commissioned.
  • A NYS criminal background check must be submitted with the cover letter bearing the signature of the Division of Criminal Justice Services representative. Federal/FBI background checks must be authenticated by the U.S. Department of State.

Do you need an apostille or a certificate of authentication?

An apostille is issued under the 1961 Hague Convention for use in countries that have joined the Convention. A Certificate of Authentication is issued for use in a country that is not a member. The official page says Certificates of Authentication “may also require an additional certification from the United States Department of State prior to submission to the foreign country.”[source]

Both certificates are described as a one-page document with a blue laser-printed facsimile of the Department of State seal and the facsimile signature of the Secretary of State or a deputy. Apostilles and certificates issued on or after April 9, 2013, may be verified through the Department’s online tool, linked from the official page.[source]
