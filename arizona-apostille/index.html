How to get an apostille in Arizona

Short answer: Send the original notarized or certified document, the Apostille/Certificate of Authentication Request form, $3 per document, and return postage to the Arizona Secretary of State, Attn: Apostille Dept., 1700 W. Washington Street, Fl. 7, Phoenix, AZ 85007-2808. Mail takes 10 to 20 business days. If you can’t wait, walk-in service costs an extra $25 expedite fee and is limited to 6 documents.[source]

The Arizona Secretary of State issues an apostille, or for other countries a certificate of authentication, to certify an Arizona notary public, court clerk, or state document custodian. Which one you get depends on the country that asked for it.[source] The Secretary of State and the Assistant Secretary of State are the officers designated to sign Arizona apostilles under the U.S. designation filed with the Hague Conference.[source]

What Arizona will and won’t apostille

  • Originals only. The office must receive an original notarized document or an original certified recorded document. A photocopy isn’t accepted.[source]
  • Arizona documents only. Don’t send a document recorded by another state; the state that produced it has to certify it. Arizona’s own example: a Nevada birth certificate goes to the Nevada Secretary of State. Out-of-state documents are returned to you.[source]
  • No electronic apostilles. Arizona issues paper certificates only.[source] The HCCH’s November 2026 e-APP chart doesn’t list an Arizona e-Register, so a recipient who wants to confirm an Arizona apostille should contact the Secretary of State directly.[source] See how to verify an apostille.
  • U.S. territories. A document going to Puerto Rico or another U.S. territory gets a notarial capacity certificate for $18, not an apostille.[source]
  • Federal documents. The Secretary of State points federal requests to the U.S. Office of Authentications.[source] Federal documents are signed by a federal official, and the U.S. designation sends them to the U.S. Department of State.[source] See apostille versus authentication.

What to send

  1. The original notarized document, or the original certified copy from the office that recorded it.[source]
  2. The completed Apostille/Certificate of Authentication Request form, naming the country where the document will be used.[source]
  3. $3 per document by check or money order to “Arizona Secretary of State.”[source]
  4. Return postage. The office recommends a trackable service. Any FedEx, UPS, DHL or other return label must show your own mailing address as both the sender and the recipient.[source]

Arizona’s page says it is a filing agency and can’t give legal advice or interpret statutes.[source] For which certificate your destination needs, see apostille by country; most Hague members simply take the apostille, and the apostille finder lays out the steps for other states’ documents.
