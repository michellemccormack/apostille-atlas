About Apostille Atlas

A reference for people who need a U.S. apostille or authentication certificate: which office issues it, what it costs, and whether you can mail the request or walk in, state by state.

Apostille Atlas is an independent comparison guide published by Atlas Guides. For questions, see Contact.

How we source things

  • Fees, office addresses, mail versus in-person rules, and processing statements come only from the official secretary of state, department of state, or other competent-authority page, with a link next to every figure.
  • The difference between an apostille and an authentication certificate is described from those official pages and from the Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH) Apostille Section.
  • If an official page does not publish a figure, such as a mail turnaround time, we show a dash or say the office does not publish one, rather than guessing.
  • Every page shows the date we last checked it. Fees and counter rules change; follow the linked official page before you file or pay.

What this site is not

It isn't legal advice, and it isn't a government office. We don't issue apostilles, notarize documents, or file requests ourselves. If you'd like someone to handle it, the request form passes your details to an independent apostille service, which quotes and does the work. You can also start a request with Apostille-USA. Destination countries can add their own rules, and some offices will not apostille a document that is missing a required earlier certification. Check the linked official page or a local attorney before you act.
