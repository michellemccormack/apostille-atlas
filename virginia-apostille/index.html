How to get an apostille in Virginia

Short answer: Virginia's Secretary of the Commonwealth issues an Apostille or Great Seal authentication for $10 per document, or $5 for each extra document signed by the same official on the same date for the same country. Every request needs an online cover letter. Mail generally takes 7 to 10 business days; in-person service is by appointment only. [source][source]

The Office of the Secretary of the Commonwealth, Authentication Division, issues Virginia’s authentications. Depending on the destination country, the certificate is either an Apostille or a Great Seal authentication. It confirms that the Virginia notary, Virginia clerk of court, or Virginia deputy state registrar is in the office’s system and notarized or issued your document correctly.[source]

Two Virginia rules catch people out. Notarized documents, vital records, court records, and State Corporation Commission documents must all be dated within the past 12 months. And Virginia cannot currently authenticate a document signed by a Virginia electronic notary (eNotary).[source]

Booking an appointment

Appointments open online only on Fridays at 10:00 a.m., for the following week only. The limit is 10 documents per customer or family, and one appointment per family member per day. Bring a valid government-issued ID (required for all adults entering the building), limit your party to two people, and print or photograph your appointment confirmation.[source]

Appointments are for individual constituents. Couriers and apostille companies must use the drop box, and those documents are returned by mail only.[source]

What will Virginia authenticate?

DocumentRequirement
Notarized documentProperly notarized by a Virginia notary within the past 12 months. Virginia does not accept documents notarized in another state.[source]
Birth, death, marriage, or divorce certificateMust be issued by the Virginia Department of Health, Vital Records (or your local DMV) within the past 12 months, and must not be notarized. Circuit Court marriage certificates need a Certification of Official Record (triple seal).[source]
Court recordIssued by the Virginia Circuit Court within the past 12 months with a Certification of Official Record (triple seal), showing the date and the clerk’s signature and printed name. Cannot be notarized.[source]
Business documentsA State Corporation Commission document issued within the past 12 months and signed by the Clerk of the Commission. To order one, call the SCC at 804-371-9733 and ask for a certified copy for international authentication.[source]

Virginia will not process federal documents, including FBI background checks, or documents notarized by military notaries. Those go to the U.S. Department of State’s Office of Authentications. It also will not process out-of-state documents, including out-of-state school transcripts and diplomas.[source]

Virginia does not require a translation before you submit, but the notarization itself must be in English.[source][source]

What does a Virginia notarization need?

The office rejects a notarized document unless it shows all of these:[source]

  1. the county or independent city where the document was signed;
  2. the date of notarization;
  3. the notarial statement saying what is being notarized (a signature, a true copy of an original, or an oath);
  4. the notary’s signature;
  5. the notary’s commission expiration date (month, day, year);
  6. the notary’s registration number;
  7. a photographically reproducible seal or stamp with the name matching the office’s records exactly.

Other common rejection reasons include a notarization more than 12 months old, a notarization split across pages, and a photocopy of a notarization.[source]

Can Virginia apostille an electronic notarization?

The Virginia authentications page says that, at this time, it cannot authenticate an electronic notarization with an Apostille or Great Seal authentication.[source] If your document is headed abroad from Virginia, have it notarized in person by a commissioned Virginia notary. The office says many banks, FedEx stores, UPS stores, and USPS locations offer notary services. Other states handle this differently; see online notarization for apostille documents.[source]
