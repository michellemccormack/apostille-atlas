Apostille offices by state

An apostille for a state document comes from that state, not from the federal government. The U.S. designation on file with the Hague Conference names the officers in each state, D.C., and several territories who can sign one.[source] In most states that is the Secretary of State; a few use the Lieutenant Governor, the Secretary of the Commonwealth, or another office.

Federal documents go to the U.S. Department of State Office of Authentications instead, and U.S. federal court documents to the clerk of that court.[source] If you'd rather have a service handle a federal (U.S. Department of State) apostille, one option is Apostille-USAaffiliate link. Checking that an apostille is genuine

The Apostille Convention lets each competent authority keep a register of the apostilles it issues. 12 of the jurisdictions above publish an online search: California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Kentucky, New York, Rhode Island, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Washington, West Virginia. For the others, contact the issuing office. Our guide to verifying an apostille has the full list of U.S. e-Registers from the Hague Conference’s November 2026 chart and the steps to follow.
