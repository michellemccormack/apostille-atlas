How to get an apostille in Florida

Short answer: Only the Florida Department of State's Division of Corporations in Tallahassee issues apostilles: $10 per document, or $20 for documents certified by a county clerk of court. Pay by check or money order; cash and cards aren't accepted. Walk-in requests are usually finished while you wait, and mail is processed in the order received. [source]

The Florida Secretary of State is the only competent authority authorized to issue notarial and apostille certifications in Florida. The Division of Corporations issues them from its Tallahassee office.

How do you file for a Florida apostille?

The official “How to File” steps are:[source]

  1. Complete the Department of State’s Apostille and Notarial Certificate Request Form.
  2. Enclose the document. An original certified copy or certificate of status of a public document is required; a photocopy is not acceptable. Notarized documents must contain a full notarial statement and the original notary’s signature, stamp, and seal.
  3. Insert a self-addressed stamped envelope, or a prepaid, pre-addressed air bill, with the requestor listed as both sender and recipient.
  4. Include payment.
  5. Mail the packet to the Apostille Section.

Do you need an apostille or a notarial certification?

An apostille is issued for use in a country that is a member of the 1961 Hague Convention. “Countries that are nonmembers of the 1961 Hague Convention are not issued an apostille; they are issued notarial certificates or certifications.” Both certificates authenticate the issuing official’s or notary’s signature.[source]

Which documents will Florida authenticate?

The official page lists, among others: Florida agency or official documents; Florida birth and death certificates; circuit court and clerk-of-the-county documents; marriage and divorce certificates; school transcripts; vehicle titles; documents notarized by a Florida notary public; Florida criminal background checks from the Florida Department of Law Enforcement or local law enforcement; and Secretary of State certified copies and certificates of status.[source]

Federal documents should be submitted to the U.S. Department of State. A document issued or notarized in another state should go to that state’s competent authority.[source]

The office does not translate documents. It also states that the person who translates a document cannot be the person who notarizes it.[source]
