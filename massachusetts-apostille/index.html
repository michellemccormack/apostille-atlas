How to get an apostille in Massachusetts

Short answer: Massachusetts apostilles come from the Secretary of the Commonwealth's Commissions Section and cost $6 per document, paid by check or money order. Mailed requests are processed within two to three weeks. The Boston counter at One Ashburton Place does up to three documents per customer; Springfield and Fall River also have offices. [source]

The Secretary of the Commonwealth, Public Records Division, Commissions Section, is the office in Massachusetts authorized to issue an apostille or certification for a document going to a foreign country. If the destination country is a signatory to the Hague Convention, the certificate is called an apostille.

What documents can Massachusetts certify?

Certifications can only be obtained for documents with an original signature of a valid Massachusetts notary public, justice of the peace, city or town clerk or assistant, court clerk or assistant, or registrar of vital records. Photocopied signatures can’t be certified.[source]

The official brochure states that the Secretary’s office is the only office in Massachusetts authorized to issue an apostille or certification for a notarized document going to a foreign country, and that Massachusetts does not certify out-of-state documents; they must be certified by the state of origin.[source]

The official request form adds that the office does not authenticate federal or out-of-state documents, and that it does not authenticate notarized photocopies of government-issued documents (its examples include a notarized photocopy of a Delaware certificate of good standing or of an FBI background check).[source]

Identify the destination country on the request. “Different countries require different types of certification. If the country is a signatory to the Hague Convention, the document will be called an apostille.”[source]
